## Where it came from

The orch suite borrows its method from US military planning. The situation check follows METT-TC (mission, enemy, terrain, troops, time, civil considerations). Candidate plans are tested by four-phase wargaming, which ends with a pre-mortem: assume the plan failed, write three failure narratives, and check each has a counteraction. The executable order uses the five-paragraph OPORD format with a Commander's Intent statement, and each important step gets an N-1 contingency, a named fallback if its primary skill is missing. Installed skills are sorted into 14 capability classes and five tiers so a plan can swap one for another.

Running a plan follows the same doctrine: known failures are adapted around by rule, unknown ones are escalated to you, one decision at a time. Planning exits early when one or two skills suffice, and a paused run is offered for resuming but never continued automatically.
